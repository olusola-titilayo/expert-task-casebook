**The payment is $888.49 a month. The true cost is an APR of about 15.85%, or an effective annual rate of about 17.06%.**

Payment on $10,000 at 1% a month for 12 months: 10,000 x 0.01 / (1 - 1.01^-12) = **$888.49**

The borrower receives only $9,800 but repays as if they'd had $10,000. The true rate is the one that makes the payments worth $9,800 today:

9,800 = sum over t = 1 to 12 of 888.49 / (1 + i)^t, which gives **i = 1.321% a month**

- APR (nominal): 1.321% x 12 = **15.85%**
- Effective annual: 1.01321^12 - 1 = **17.06%**

**Why it's different:** the $200 fee is an extra cost on money the borrower never gets. It weighs heavily because the balance falls every month, so the average amount borrowed is only a little over half of the loan. The fee adds nearly 4 percentage points, not the 2 its headline suggests.
